Adventure Works is a large, multinational manufacturing company that produces and distributes bicycles, parts, and accessories for commercial markets in North America, Europe, and Asia. The company employs 500 workers. Additionally, Adventure Works employs several regional sales teams throughout its market base. Your project is to create a tabular model for sales and marketing users to analyze Internet sales data in the AdventureWorksDW database.

AdventureWorks, a global manufacturing company, is facing challenges in making data-driven decisions due to a lack of structured financial reporting and performance tracking. Without a clear view of key financial metrics such as sales, revenue, and profit, the company struggles to assess regional performance, identify product-level trends, and recognize high-value products. This lack of visibility hinders strategic planning and operational efficiency.


To support strategic decision historical sales data was analyzed from 2011-2014 across several keys areas 

Product & Category Analytics: Pareto Analysis (80/20 Rule): Identifying the vital few categories driving the bulk of revenue.
Product Margin Matrix: Plotting categories by volume vs. profitability to identify "cash cows" vs. "duds."
Revenue Concentration/Risk Analysis: Assessing over-reliance on specific types of products (e.g., low-margin accessories).


Mix Shift & Variance Analysis: Covered by: Questions 1 and 3
Specific Frameworks:
Price/Volume/Mix (PVM) Decomposition: This is the core of Q3. You aren't just asking "what is the margin gap?" You are asking "how much of this gap is driven by Pricing (wholesale discounts) vs. Mix (B2B buying different items than B2C)?"
Channel Mix Analysis: (Q1) Understanding if a shift in where customers buy (e.g., Amazon vs. Direct Web) caused a macro-level margin collapse, rather than a shift in what they buy.


. Channel & Route-to-Market Analytics
Covered by: Questions 3 and 4
Specific Frameworks:
Channel P&L Modeling: Looking at B2B vs. B2C not just as revenue streams, but as distinct business models with different cost structures.
Account Ranking & Segmentation: (Q4) Moving from a "revenue-centric" view of partners to a "value-centric" view.
4. Customer & Account Profitability (Cost-to-Serve)
Covered by: Question 4
Specific Frameworks:
Cost-to-Serve (CTS) Analysis: This is a deep dive into the hidden costs of doing business. Are certain B2B resellers demanding custom packaging, net-90 payment terms, or massive freight allowances?
Net Margin vs. Gross Margin Analytics: Stripping away the "top-line illusion" to see what actually drops to the bottom line per account.
